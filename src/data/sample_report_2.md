### Urban Heat Island Mitigation with Green Infrastructure

Assess Cities are increasingly becoming islands of heat → a phenomenon now widely known as Urban Heat Islands (UHIs. This is far more than a simple meteorological quirk; it is a genuine environmental and social stressor. UHIs cause urban temperatures to remain significantly higher than those in surrounding rural areas, a difference created by the materials that make up the cityscape itself. Concrete and asphalt soak up solar radiation far more efficiently than natural land, creating pockets of warmth that can sit several degrees higher than nearby green space. This thermal profile isn’t constant; it shifts with the time of day, current weather, and the specific architecture of the streets. Replacing fields and forests with impervious surfaces removes the cooling effects of natural evaporation.

By adding natural surfaces like vegetation back into communities, green infrastructure can mitigate the heat island effect and provide cooling.
